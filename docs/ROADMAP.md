# Roadmap and source expansion

## Public-first sequence

1. **Current US/global registry slice** — ClinicalTrials.gov + registered PubMed references (this
   repository).
2. **Scientific outcome evidence** — rights-aware open full text, registry result tables,
   correction/retraction events and typed outcome comparison.
3. **Public funding** — NIH RePORTER and other jurisdictional grants, joined only for requested
   trials/candidates.
4. **Candidate and organization identity** — aliases, licences, acquisitions and legal entities;
   then filings/regulatory/court events with procedural states.
5. **Jurisdiction packs** — UK, Australia, EU/EEA and other registries only through supported,
   rights-compatible interfaces, with per-source coverage declarations.
6. **Watches and compact DICE snapshots** — what changed, with prior public answer reproducibility.

## Public/private model

The free service can answer from public sources and make its gaps visible. A business can connect
licensed sources (for example commercial trial/candidate intelligence and bibliographic indexes)
or private protocols, statistical analysis plans, clinical-study reports, CTMS/eTMF and disclosure
correspondence. Entitlements must flow through every derived result, cache and DICE proposition so
private/licensed evidence cannot leak into a public answer.

The commercial pitch is not “pay to remove citations.” It is: keep the same governed question and
evidence contract, then add the missing evidence the organization is allowed to see.

## Countries are separate coverage claims

ClinicalTrials.gov includes many non-US locations but is not a complete national registry for
Australia or the UK. Planned adapters should retain duplicate/secondary identifiers and declare
source-specific coverage:

- Australia/New Zealand: ANZCTR through a supported interface or partnership; TGA evidence is a
  separate regulatory relation, not proof of trial registration completeness.
- UK: ISRCTN and HRA/other official sources subject to their interface and reuse terms.
- EU/EEA: CTIS plus legacy-record reconciliation.
- Global: WHO ICTRP only under compatible terms; do not silently bulk-ingest it.

## Company context, subordinate to the disease answer

A selected trial may resolve its sponsor string to a legal entity and then show headquarters,
parents/subsidiaries, other trials, candidate ownership events, filings, regulatory actions,
market observations and litigation. Each relation requires independent evidence. A complaint is
an allegation; dismissal, settlement, judgment and appeal are different events. Market cap needs
venue, currency, price time and share basis and must never feed efficacy classification.

## Thought bubble: funding and political conflicts of interest

A future accountability Lens could ask whether political decision-makers received disclosed
donations from companies connected to trials, products, public awards or regulatory decisions.
This is potentially valuable and also unusually easy to overstate.

The graph would need time-bounded, jurisdiction-specific links among legal company entities,
corporate PACs/affiliates, candidates and trials; recipients, committees and office terms;
donations; lobbying disclosures; public awards; regulator decisions; and the official disclosure
source. The answer should render “a disclosed donation from X to Y during interval Z,” not “X bought
influence.” Identity resolution, reporting thresholds, intermediary entities, refunds, in-kind
support, currency and election-law changes must remain visible. Causal or conflict conclusions
require an explicit rule, relevant policy/law, and ideally human review.

This is deliberately a thought bubble, not part of the current build.
